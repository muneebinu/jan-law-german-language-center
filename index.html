Course curriculum

Explore the main topics, grammar and classroom practice covered at A1, A2 and B1.

BEGINNER CEFR A1

German A1

Start from the beginning and build the language foundations you need for simple, everyday communication.

Topics and vocabulary

  • Alphabet, pronunciation, greetings and introductions
  • Personal details, family, friends and describing people
  • Numbers, dates, days, months, clock time and appointments
  • Home, rooms, food, drinks, shopping and prices
  • Daily routines, hobbies, free time and simple directions

Grammar and sentence building

  • Personal pronouns and the verbs sein and haben
  • Present tense, regular and common irregular verbs
  • Statements, yes/no questions and W-questions
  • Nominative and introductory accusative, articles and plurals
  • Possessives, negation with nicht and kein, and basic modal verbs
  • Word order and simple separable verbs

What you will practise

Introduce yourself, ask and answer simple questions, fill in basic forms, understand short messages and write short personal texts.

By the end of A1Handle familiar everyday expressions and simple conversations when people speak clearly and slowly.
ELEMENTARY CEFR A2

German A2

Expand your vocabulary and learn to communicate in more situations at home, at work and while travelling.

Topics and vocabulary

  • Workplaces, jobs, education and everyday responsibilities
  • Travel, transport, holidays, accommodation and asking for directions
  • Health, body, appointments and describing common problems
  • Shopping, clothing, services, food and making plans
  • Past events, experiences, invitations and social activities

Grammar and sentence building

  • Perfekt for talking about past events and common Präteritum forms
  • Modal verbs, separable verbs and reflexive verbs
  • Accusative and dative cases with common verbs and prepositions
  • Two-way prepositions in common location and movement contexts
  • Comparative and superlative forms, and adjective use
  • Subordinate clauses with weil, dass and wenn

What you will practise

Describe routines and past experiences, make appointments, ask for help, give directions, write short messages and manage common travel or shopping situations.

By the end of A2Communicate in routine situations and exchange information about familiar topics using connected, simple sentences.
INTERMEDIATE CEFR B1

German B1

Develop greater independence, explain your ideas and take part in conversations about familiar subjects.

Topics and vocabulary

  • Education, careers, workplace communication and future plans
  • Travel, culture, media, technology and everyday news
  • Health, lifestyle, environment and community life
  • Personal experiences, relationships, events and memorable moments
  • Opinions, advantages and disadvantages, problems and possible solutions

Grammar and sentence building

  • Past tenses and choosing between Perfekt and Präteritum
  • Subordinate clauses with obwohl, während, damit and other connectors
  • Relative clauses and indirect questions
  • Konjunktiv II for polite requests, suggestions and hypothetical situations
  • Infinitive clauses with zu and common da-compounds
  • Adjective endings, passive voice and more advanced word order

What you will practise

Give and support opinions, compare options, explain reasons, describe experiences, write structured emails and take part in discussions and short presentations.

By the end of B1Understand the main points of clear standard communication and deal with many familiar situations independently.
PRACTICE THROUGHOUT ALL LEVELS

Skills practised in class

Language develops through regular practice, not grammar alone. Lessons bring the main communication skills together at a level-appropriate pace.

LesenReading short texts, notices, messages and level-appropriate passages.
HörenListening to conversations, announcements and everyday spoken German.
SchreibenBuilding sentences and writing messages, emails and short texts.
SprechenPractising introductions, questions, role-plays, descriptions and discussions.

The order of topics may vary according to the pace of the class and students’ learning needs.
